How it works

The checks follow the method of commercial integrity checkers: fingerprint the text, search the databases, group every match, and score AI writing sentence by sentence.

1

Fingerprinting

The text is read with its exact position on every page, split into words, sentences and paragraphs, and cut into overlapping five-word fingerprints. Look-alike letters from other alphabets are swapped back first, so they cannot hide a match.

2

Searching the databases

Phrases from the paper are searched in Wikipedia, OpenAlex, Crossref, Europe PMC and Semantic Scholar (and, with your key, the whole web through Google Programmable Search). Every earlier submission is searched too.

3

Scoring similarity

Overall Similarity is the share of the paper's words matched to any source. Top Sources hands each matched word to the strongest source first, so overlapping sources are not counted twice. The bibliography and quoted text can be filtered out.

4

Detecting AI writing

Only qualifying prose counts: headings, lists, tables and the bibliography are left out. Each sentence is scored in overlapping windows of its neighbours from signals such as word predictability, sentence-length burstiness, stock transitions and citation habits. At least 300 words are needed, and scores from 1% to 19% show as *%.

Match groups

  • Not Cited or Quoted: matches with neither in-text citation nor quotation marks.
  • Missing Quotations: cited text so close to the source that it may also need quotation marks.
  • Missing Citation: text in quotation marks with no in-text citation.
  • Cited and Quoted: matches with both a citation and quotation marks.

Integrity flags

Replaced Characters: letters swapped for similar characters from another alphabet. Hidden Text: invisible characters inside words, or text too small to see. A flag is a prompt to look closer, not proof of a problem.

The two reports

Every scan produces the same two PDFs, page for page, whatever the document.

Similarity Report

  1. Cover Page: author, title, institution, submission ID and dates, file name and size, pages, words and characters.
  2. Integrity Overview: Overall Similarity, filters, the four match groups, the share from internet sources, publications and submitted works, and the integrity flags.
  3. Top Sources: every source, numbered and colour-coded, with its type and share.
  4. Integrity Submission: every page of the paper with matches highlighted in the source's colour and source numbers in the margin.

AI Writing Report

  1. Cover Page: the same document details.
  2. AI Writing Overview: the percentage detected as AI, the number of AI-generated segments, and the caution and disclaimer.
  3. AI Writing Submission: every page of the paper with the likely AI-generated text highlighted.

Questions

Is my document uploaded anywhere?

No. The file is read and the reports are built on your device. Only short phrases from the text are sent as search queries to the databases turned on in Settings. Submissions are kept in this browser's repository, which you can clear at any time.

Which files can I check?

PDF, Word (.docx), plain text, Markdown and RTF, or pasted text. Scanned images need text recognition (OCR) first.

Can a teacher check a whole class?

Yes. Add every paper at once: each one is checked against the others and against everything submitted before on the same device.

Why can scores differ from other checkers?

Each checker searches its own databases. Commercial services index billions of web pages and private student-paper collections that no public database offers, and their AI models are private, so results on the same paper can differ.

What does *% mean in the AI Writing Report?

An AI score from 1% to 19% is shown as *% with no highlights, because low scores have a higher chance of false positives.
